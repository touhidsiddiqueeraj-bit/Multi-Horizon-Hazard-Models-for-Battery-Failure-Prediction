Dear Editors,

We are pleased to submit our manuscript "Multi-Horizon Failure-Risk Models
for Battery Failure Prediction: How Much Cross-Chemistry Transfer Is a
State-of-Health Shortcut?" for consideration as a full-length research
article in the International Journal of Prognostics and Health Management.

The manuscript reports a validity-focused study of multi-horizon battery
failure prediction. Its central contribution is a controlled diagnosis of
apparent cross-dataset, cross-chemistry transferability: through minimal
baselines, factorial feature and failure-definition ablations, and
same-chemistry cross-laboratory controls, we show that much of the
apparent transfer is a State-of-Health shortcut that partially reflects
the label definition itself, rather than transferable degradation
knowledge. The study also contributes a discrete-time hazard formulation
whose cumulative risks are monotone in the horizon by construction, a
leakage-free cross-fitted calibration protocol, cell-level bootstrap
inference, and an operational cost analysis.
