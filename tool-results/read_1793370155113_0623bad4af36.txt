     1→    80→
     2→    81→The objective of this study is to close the three gaps identified above and, in doing so, to answer the validity question rigorously. The contributions are:
     3→    82→\begin{itemize}
     4→    83→\item a four-model benchmark (three tree ensembles plus a GRU) on two LCO datasets under cell-disjoint splits with cross-fitted, leakage-free calibration;
     5→    84→\item a discrete-time hazard formulation whose survival product is monotone in the horizon by construction, against independent fixed-horizon classifiers that violate monotonicity on 32--45\% of rows (see Supplementary Material);
     6→    85→\item a shortcut diagnosis of cross-dataset transfer: minimal baselines, a factorial feature ablation, a failure-definition ablation relabeling failures by SOH or voltage sag alone, and same-chemistry cross-laboratory controls separating dataset shift from chemistry shift;
     7→    86→\item a cell-as-unit uncertainty treatment: cell-level bootstrap intervals on every headline number, with DeLong tests demoted to secondary evidence;
     8→    87→\item a calibration analysis under shift with small-sample recalibration, a cost-sensitive operational evaluation, and a complete ESP32-S3 embedded deployment.
     9→    88→\end{itemize}

    13→    92→\section{Methodology}
    14→    93→\subsection{Composite Failure Label}
    15→    94→Following the original protocol \cite{shikdar2026learning}, cell State-of-Health at cycle $k$ is discharge capacity over mean first-10-cycle capacity,
    16→    95→\begin{equation}\label{eq:soh}
    17→    96→\begin{aligned}
    18→    97→\mathrm{SOH}_k^{(c)} &= \frac{Q_k^{(c)}}{\bar{Q}^{(c)}},\\[2pt]
    19→    98→\bar{Q}^{(c)} &= \frac{1}{10}\sum_{i=1}^{10} Q_i^{(c)},
    20→    99→\end{aligned}
    21→   100→\end{equation}
    22→   101→where $Q_k^{(c)}$ is the discharge capacity at cycle $k$. The voltage baseline $\bar{V}^{(c)}$ is defined analogously. A discharge cycle $t$ in cell $c$ is labeled as failing within horizon $H$ if any cycle $k\in[t,t{+}H)$ meets either of two criteria,
    23→   102→\begin{equation}\label{eq:label}
    24→   103→y_t^{(c,H)} \;=\; \begin{cases}
    25→   104→1, & \exists\, k\in[t,t{+}H):\ \mathrm{SOH}_k^{(c)}\le 0.80 \ \text{or}\\[2pt]
    26→   105→& \bar{V}_k^{(c)} < 0.94\,\bar{V}^{(c)},\\[2pt]
    27→   106→0, & \text{otherwise,}
    28→   107→\end{cases}
    29→   108→\end{equation}
    30→   109→and once triggered the label stays 1 for all later cycles. Because the window $[t,t{+}H)$ includes the current cycle, rows scored at or after the failure crossing are labeled positive for every formulation in this paper: with-SOH results therefore mix concurrent failure detection with future failure prediction, and Section~III-C reports the two components separately. The task is to estimate the cumulative failure risk over the horizon,
    31→   110→\begin{equation}\label{eq:risk}
    32→   111→p_t^{(c,H)} \;=\; \Pr\!\bigl(T_f \le t{+}H \mid T_f > t,\ \mathbf{x}_t^{(c)}\bigr),
    33→   112→\end{equation}
    34→   113→where $T_f$ is the failure cycle and $\mathbf{x}_t^{(c)}$ the feature vector at cycle $t$. Datasets without voltage data, or with voltage at the cutoff as in Oxford's flat LFP plateau, fall back to SOH alone.
    35→   114→
    36→   115→Three properties of this label definition matter for everything that follows. First, \eqref{eq:risk} is a fixed-horizon cumulative failure risk, not a discrete-time hazard: each horizon is an independent binary classifier with no monotonicity constraint $p_t^{(c,10)}\le p_t^{(c,20)}\le p_t^{(c,30)}\le p_t^{(c,50)}$. Section~II-E adds a true discrete-time hazard model, and the Supplementary Material quantifies the violations. Second, the label is \emph{partially defined by SOH itself}, and SOH is also an input feature; a with-SOH model can therefore detect proximity to the SOH $=0.80$ threshold without learning degradation dynamics. Every with-SOH result here is an upper bound including this leakage, and Section~III-E measures it by relabeling failures one endpoint at a time. Third, the endpoints separate: the label can use the SOH criterion alone, voltage sag alone, or both, independently of the feature ablation.

    65→   144→
    66→   145→\subsection{Features and Preprocessing}
    67→   146→Each cycle is described by seven scalar features: cycle index, average and minimum discharge voltage, average discharge current, average temperature, discharge duration, and SOH. Trees are scale-invariant and need no standardization; the GRU and logistic baselines are standardized with training-set statistics. Unlogged CALCE and Battery Archive columns (temperature, duration, current) are zero-filled; a constant-zero column cannot split within one dataset but acts as a dataset identifier in pooled training, a confound controlled with a common-feature subset (Section~III-C).
    68→   147→
    69→   148→\subsection{Models}
    70→   149→Three tree ensembles are benchmarked with hyperparameters matched to the original study \cite{shikdar2026learning}: XGBoost \cite{chen2016xgboost} with max\_depth 4, 300 estimators, learning rate 0.05, subsample 0.8, colsample\_bytree 0.8, min\_child\_weight 5; LightGBM \cite{ke2017lightgbm} with the same depth, count, and rate; and Random Forest \cite{breiman2001random} with max\_depth 6 and 300 estimators; all random\_state 42.
    71→   150→
    72→   151→A compact GRU \cite{cho2014learning} tests whether sequence-aware representations transfer across chemistries. It ingests sliding windows of $W{=}10$ cycles per cell through one 8-unit GRU layer,
    73→   152→\begin{equation}\label{eq:gru}
    74→   153→\begin{aligned}
    75→   154→\mathbf{r}_t &= \sigma(W_r\mathbf{x}_t + U_r\mathbf{h}_{t-1}),\quad
    76→   155→\mathbf{z}_t = \sigma(W_z\mathbf{x}_t + U_z\mathbf{h}_{t-1}),\\
    77→   156→\tilde{\mathbf{h}}_t &= \tanh(W_h\mathbf{x}_t + U_h(\mathbf{r}_t\odot\mathbf{h}_{t-1})),\\
    78→   157→\mathbf{h}_t &= (1-\mathbf{z}_t)\odot\mathbf{h}_{t-1} + \mathbf{z}_t\odot\tilde{\mathbf{h}}_t,
    79→   158→\end{aligned}
    80→   159→\end{equation}
    81→   160→and the final hidden state maps through a sigmoid linear layer to the failure probability (Adam, learning rate 0.005, binary cross-entropy). The 8-unit width matches the small training-cell counts. For cross-dataset runs the feature set is reduced to the common, unit-safe set --- cycle number, average and minimum voltage, plus SOH when enabled --- and the trees are evaluated on the identical common set, so no architecture claim rests on a feature-confounded comparison.
    82→   161→
    83→   162→Because the central question is whether apparent performance is a health-state shortcut, the most important models are embarrassingly simple ones \cite{shikdar2026learning}: the fitted-free \emph{SOH distance rule} $d_t = 0.80 - \mathrm{SOH}_t$; \emph{SOH-only}, \emph{cycle-only}, \emph{SOH+cycle}, \emph{sensors-only}, and \emph{full-feature} logistic regressions. If the SOH-only and distance-rule baselines reproduce most of the transferable performance of the tuned ensembles, then the ensembles contribute little beyond reading the health state --- the negative result this validity analysis predicts.
    84→   163→
    85→   164→\subsection{A True Discrete-Time Hazard Model}
    86→   165→To complement the four independent fixed-horizon classifiers, we also fit an explicit discrete-time survival model \cite{cox1972regression,kalbfleisch2002statistical}. For each observed cycle $t$ and offset $\ell\in\{1,\dots,L\}$ with $L{=}50$, define the hazard
    87→   166→\begin{equation}\label{eq:hazard}
    88→   167→h_\ell(t) \;=\; \Pr\!\bigl(T_f = t{+}\ell \mid T_f \ge t{+}\ell,\ \mathbf{x}_t\bigr),
    89→   168→\end{equation}
    90→   169→conditional on the features measured at $t$ only. One pooled classifier --- XGBoost, and a logistic regression --- is fit on the offset-expanded at-risk rows $(\mathbf{x}_t, \ell)$ with target $\mathbf{1}\{T_f = t{+}\ell\}$, restricted to $t{+}\ell \le T_f$, with $\ell$ as an input feature. The cumulative risk over horizon $H$ is the survival product
    91→   170→\begin{equation}\label{eq:survival}
    92→   171→R(t,H) \;=\; 1 - \prod_{\ell=1}^{H}\bigl(1-\hat h_\ell(t)\bigr),
    93→   172→\end{equation}
    94→   173→which is monotone non-decreasing in $H$ by construction and yields all four horizons from one model. Rows at or after failure are scored exactly like the fixed-horizon classifiers score them, so the evaluation rows are identical across formulations.
    95→   174→
    96→   175→\subsection{Calibration Protocol}
    97→   176→Three post-hoc calibration schemes are compared: Platt scaling fits a sigmoid to the raw scores $s_i$,
    98→   177→\begin{equation}\label{eq:platt}
    99→   178→p_i \;=\; \frac{1}{1+\exp(-\alpha s_i - \beta)},
   100→   179→\end{equation}
   101→   180→by logistic regression; isotonic regression \cite{zadrozny2002transforming} fits the non-decreasing step function that minimizes squared error; and temperature scaling divides the logit of the score by a single fitted temperature \cite{guo2017calibration}.
   102→   181→
   103→   182→The protocol is leakage-clean. For every within-dataset fold, calibrators are fitted on \emph{cross-fitted out-of-fold scores} from an inner cell-disjoint GroupKFold over the training cells; untouched test cells are scored by a model trained on all training cells. In transfer, calibrators are fitted on source out-of-fold scores only. No calibrator is ever fitted on the scores it is applied to, and no test cell contributes to training or calibration. A reproducibility note is retained from the original study comparison: the original study \cite{shikdar2026learning} reported Brier scores around 0.032; re-execution yields roughly 0.09--0.29, a gap of unknown cause that does not affect the relative comparisons forming the core of this paper.
   104→   183→
   105→   184→\subsection{Transfer Protocols}
   106→   185→Transfer is evaluated by training on LCO cells (NASA alone, CALCE alone, or both) and testing on the target cells, reported per cell and pooled with cell-level bootstrap intervals \cite{efron1979bootstrap}; pooling alone can hide degenerate constant-score behavior, which per-cell statistics expose. Three families of transfer are used: \textbf{cross-chemistry} (LCO$\to$LFP): NASA, CALCE, or both $\to$ Oxford and $\to$ Severson, with and without SOH, and on the common feature set shared with the GRU; \textbf{same-chemistry, cross-laboratory controls}: NASA$\to$CALCE, CALCE$\to$NASA (LCO$\to$LCO) and Severson$\to$Oxford, Oxford$\to$Severson (LFP$\to$LFP), which separate dataset shift from chemistry shift; and \textbf{feature-definition ablations}: the factorial feature grid and the failure-endpoint relabeling of Section~III-E.
   107→   186→
   108→   187→\subsection{Evaluation and Statistical Testing}
   109→   188→\label{sec:eval}
   110→   189→Within-dataset evaluation uses 5-fold cell-grouped cross-validation on NASA (37 cells) and leave-one-cell-out on CALCE (7 cells): splits are cell-disjoint, models retrained per horizon. The experimental unit is the cell: headline AUCs \cite{hanley1982meaning} are fold means with fold SDs, every headline carries a 95\% cell-resampling interval (2\,000 draws; 400 for Severson), and paired comparisons reuse the same resamples. The DeLong nonparametric test \cite{delong1988comparing} is retained as secondary evidence: it treats every cycle-level prediction as independent, so its $p$-values are inflated by pseudoreplication and are read only as directional consistency checks.
   111→   190→
   112→   191→Discrimination alone is not enough for a reliability-oriented study, so each within-dataset configuration also reports the Brier score \cite{brier1950verification}, expected calibration error (10 equal-width bins) \cite{guo2017calibration}, log loss, the slope and intercept of a logistic recalibration of the predicted probabilities, the area under the precision--recall curve (PR-AUC) \cite{saito2015precision}, and sensitivity at a fixed 10\% false-positive rate. Three reporting conventions apply throughout. Pooled AUC is dominated by long-lived cells, so per-cell statistics and cell-level bootstrap intervals are the primary evidence. Failure prevalence rises with the horizon, so Brier score, calibration error, thresholds, and precision--recall are horizon-dependent. The cost model prices a missed failure at 20$\times$ a false alarm; thresholds use a 10\% source false-alarm budget.
   113→   192→
   114→   193→\subsection{Embedded Deployment Architecture}
   115→   194→The deployment pipeline exports the three trained models into one flat binary run on an ESP32-S3 --- a microcontroller-class TinyML target \cite{banbury2021mlperf} --- by a hand-written C tree walker: a 12-byte header, a $3{\times}4$-byte offset table, and per-model sections of consecutive node arrays (split feature, float threshold, child indices; leaves store class-1 probabilities). Writing $\ell_t$ for the leaf value reached in tree $t$ and $T$ for the number of trees, Random Forest averages and clamps its leaf probabilities,
   116→   195→\begin{equation}\label{eq:rf}
   117→   196→p \;=\; \mathrm{clamp}\!\left(\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\ell_t,\ 0,\ 1\right),
   118→   197→\end{equation}
   119→   198→while XGBoost and LightGBM pass the summed score through a sigmoid,
   120→   199→\begin{equation}\label{eq:sigmoid}
   121→   200→p \;=\; \frac{1}{1+\exp\!\bigl(-\textstyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}\ell_t - s_0\bigr)},
   122→   201→\end{equation}
   123→   202→where $s_0$ is the model's initialization score ($s_0\neq 0$ only for XGBoost). Correctness is confirmed in three stages --- a Python walker mirroring the C logic against \texttt{predict\_proba()}, a cross-compiled x86\_64 binary, and on-target ESP32-S3 execution --- all below $10^{-5}$ maximum absolute error before deployment is accepted.

   169→   248→\subsection{Calibration Under Cross-Fitting}
   170→   249→Table~\ref{tab:cal} compares the three calibrators, averaged over the tree models and horizons, where every calibrator is fitted on cross-fitted out-of-fold scores. Three observations follow. First, on discrimination the raw scores are effectively unbeatable within dataset: any fitted calibrator is a monotone re-mapping and preserves per-fold ranking up to the ties it introduces, and the fold-mean AUC differences between calibrators are correspondingly small. Second, the step function of isotonic regression creates large tied groups whose cost is invisible to fold-mean ROC-AUC but severe for precision--recall: on NASA, raw scores reach a mean PR-AUC of 0.774 while isotonic collapses to 0.639 (Supplementary Material). Third, calibration quality proper is best for Platt on CALCE (ECE 0.029 against 0.049 for isotonic), but the fitted Platt slope on CALCE is only 0.011: in that long-tailed, leave-one-cell-out setting the out-of-fold sigmoid essentially washes confidence out toward the base rate while preserving ranking. Temperature scaling is the most balanced compromise on NASA (slope 1.218, Brier 0.178, both better than Platt's). There is no global winner across datasets, so we read the comparison as: \emph{Platt preserves discrimination best while achieving calibration error comparable to or better than isotonic} --- a materially weaker and more defensible claim than ``Platt is the better calibrator'' --- and raw scores remain the safest ranking signal whenever the operating context is uncertain.

   192→   271→\subsection{Cross-Chemistry Transfer and the SOH Ablation}
   193→   272→Table~\ref{tab:crosswith} reports transfer from the LCO sources to the two LFP targets with SOH available, and Table~\ref{tab:crossno} repeats the identical protocol with SOH removed from the feature set (both at $H{=}20$; Fig.~\ref{fig:collapse}). With SOH, transfer looks strong: pooled AUC spans 0.85--1.00 on Oxford and 0.85--0.90 on Severson, with per-cell means of 0.76--1.00. Removing SOH collapses every configuration toward or below chance: Oxford falls to 0.33--0.58 pooled AUC and Severson to 0.53--0.75. The paired cell-level bootstrap puts the with-minus-without difference on Severson at $+0.146$ [0.097, 0.233] for XGBoost at $H{=}20$, with positive intervals at every horizon for all three ensembles (deltas 0.10--0.18); Oxford point estimates agree ($+0.26$ to $+0.64$), but paired intervals are undefined with five cells, so Oxford contributes directional consistency only. On Severson the pooled numbers are dominated by long-lived cells; per-cell means without SOH remain as high as 0.88 (ALL-LCO Random Forest), so individual-cell ranking partially survives even where pooled transfer fails --- the two views bracket what actually transfers.
   194→   273→
   195→   274→Three further observations tighten the attribution. First, the detection/prediction split of Section~II-A: because the labeling window includes the current cycle, with-SOH scores mix concurrent detection with future prediction. On Severson, 99.2\% of positive rows are scored before the failure crossing, and removing the remaining at-or-after-crossing rows drops with-SOH transfer from 0.896 to 0.838 [0.760, 0.991] and no-SOH transfer from 0.750 to 0.721 [0.666, 0.833] (ALL-LCO XGBoost); the unfitted distance rule drops from 0.912 to 0.862. With-SOH numbers are therefore diagnostic-proximity upper bounds rather than pure prognosis. Second, the compact GRU, evaluated on the common four-feature subset with three seeds, is not exempt: without SOH its per-cell transfer to Severson is 0.987$\pm$0.015 from the NASA source yet 0.479$\pm$0.451 from CALCE (individual seeds span 0.21--1.00) and 0.893$\pm$0.131 from ALL-LCO --- sequence representations can succeed where trees fail, but not reproducibly enough to carry a transfer claim. Third, calibration: isotonic maps fitted on source out-of-fold scores cost the tree ensembles up to 0.48 pooled AUC on Oxford and 0.31 on Severson under transfer even with SOH present, while Platt's smooth sigmoid degrades more gracefully (Section~III-H).

   252→   331→\subsection{Minimal Baselines and the Factorial Feature Ablation}
   253→   332→Table~\ref{tab:baselines} places the tuned ensembles next to trivial baselines. The distance-to-threshold rule $d_t = 0.80 - \mathrm{SOH}_t$, which involves no fitting at all, transfers to Severson at pooled AUC 0.912 [0.836, 0.998] for every LCO source --- matching or exceeding the tuned ensembles' with-SOH transfer in Table~\ref{tab:crosswith}. An SOH-only logistic regression behaves equivalently (0.912). Both are diagnostic baselines by construction: they order cells by proximity to the label threshold, not by learned degradation dynamics. By contrast, the sensor-only logistic regression transfers at 0.301 on Severson (NASA source): below chance, meaning the remaining sensor features actively invert on the shifted distribution. Within CALCE, the cycle index alone ranks failure at pooled AUC 0.936, approaching the tuned ensembles, because CALCE's seven cells span very different cycle lives; on NASA, whose cells age under randomized profiles, the same feature is inverted (0.354).
   254→   333→
   255→   334→The full $2^3-1$ factorial grid over \{SOH, cycle index, sensors\} at $H{=}20$ sharpens the diagnosis (Supplementary Material). SOH-only is the strongest single group on both LFP targets, confirming that the health coordinate dominates whatever transfer exists. Cycle-only is competitive on Oxford in the logistic baselines (0.99, NASA source) --- whose five cells fail at well-separated raw cycle counts, so the cycle index is a between-cell aging proxy --- but not in the factorial XGBoost grid itself (0.54 on Oxford, 0.71 on Severson): the pattern is model- and source-specific, not portable degradation knowledge. No combination without SOH reaches 0.76 on either target (best 0.75), and removing both SOH and the cycle index leaves the sensors with essentially nothing (0.535 with the ALL-LCO source on Severson). The ablation makes the shortcut diagnosis feature-by-feature: it is not that the models fail to use the sensor features; it is that the sensor features themselves carry no cross-dataset signal.
   256→   335→
   257→   336→Three conclusions follow. First, most of what transfers is the SOH coordinate, not degradation knowledge. Second, ``more features'' does not help transfer; sensor features carry dataset-specific artifacts that can invert. Third, the ensembles' within-dataset value is undiminished --- but the transfer claim must be downgraded from ``models transfer'' to ``the health state transfers, and models can read it.''

   302→   381→\subsection{Same-Chemistry Cross-Laboratory Controls}
   303→   382→If the collapse of Section~III-C were a chemistry effect, transfer should survive when chemistry is held constant. Table~\ref{tab:samechem} shows it does not. NASA$\to$CALCE (both LCO) retains most of its transfer without SOH, so some laboratory pairs do share portable sensor signal; but Severson$\to$Oxford (both LFP) reproduces the full collapse pattern of the cross-chemistry shifts, including high with-SOH transfer and near-chance sensor-only transfer. These controls tighten the central attribution: the failure to transfer is a property of \emph{dataset shift} --- laboratories, protocols, and logging conventions --- that chemistry change adds to but does not uniquely cause. SOH is therefore a \emph{dataset- and chemistry-dependent} health-state signal, not a chemistry-specific one, and chemistry is not isolated as a causal variable.

   322→   401→\subsection{Discrete-Time Hazard Model and Monotonicity}
   323→   402→Within datasets the survival product of Section~II-E matches the independent fixed-horizon classifiers to within a few AUC points at every horizon (Table~\ref{tab:within}); no formal equivalence test is run. Under transfer the survival product inherits the same SOH dependence --- the ALL-LCO hazard XGBoost transfers to Oxford at pooled AUC 0.314, significantly below chance, and the hazard logistic degenerates to exactly 0.500 on three of six source--target pairs --- so model formulation does not exempt inputs from dataset shift. What the hazard formulation buys is structural: $R(t,H)$ is monotone in $H$ by construction, whereas the four independent classifiers violate $p_{10}\le p_{20}\le p_{30}\le p_{50}$ on 32--45\% of within-dataset rows (raw scores; 5.6--26\% after Platt calibration), rising to 95.8\% on transferred Severson rows (NASA-trained XGBoost). Monotone cumulative risk is what makes ``risk within 50 cycles'' coherent to quote: the hazard model buys structural coherence and a single fit at no within-dataset cost, and pays the same SOH shortcut as every other model. The full monotonicity table is in the Supplementary Material.

   325→   404→\subsection{Calibration Transfer and Its Partial Repair}
   326→   405→A second, independent failure mode appears when calibrated outputs are required under transfer. Isotonic regression is fitted as a step function on the source distribution; when target scores shift, many target scores fall into one bin and receive identical calibrated probabilities, and pooled ranking degrades accordingly --- isotonic calibration costs the tree ensembles up to 0.48 pooled AUC on Oxford and 0.31 on Severson even with SOH present (Section~III-C). Platt's smooth sigmoid degrades more gracefully, which is precisely the sense in which Platt better preserves discrimination at comparable calibration error (Section~III-B). On the shifted target, no source-fitted calibrator lands near the diagonal, however it is fitted.
   327→   406→
   328→   407→The recalibration arms of the companion study \cite{shikdar2026robustness} ask whether a small labeled target sample repairs this. Arm~A refits a calibrator on $k$ labeled target cells while leaving the classifier's scores untouched; Arm~B continues training the classifier on them. On Severson without SOH, zero-shot ECE is 0.817; at $k{=}5$ labeled cells, isotonic and Platt reduce it to 0.044 and 0.045, while temperature scaling remains poorly calibrated (0.563). Calibration error is repaired, but discrimination is not: mean pooled AUC at $k{=}5$ stays near 0.61, because the calibrators create ties or reverse rankings on the shifted target. Arm~B is source-dependent: with NASA-trained XGBoost and $k{=}5$, Severson AUC rises from 0.620 to 0.854, overshooting the within-LCO reference ceiling (ratio 1.04, an artifact of the mismatched ceiling rather than genuine super-ceiling recovery), while CALCE-trained updates leave the source operating point essentially untouched (LCO-holdout retention 0.997 to 0.996) and ALL-LCO updates damage it substantially (0.690 to 0.567). Both arms are tabulated in the Supplementary Material.
