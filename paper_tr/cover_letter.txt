Dear Editor-in-Chief,

We submit our manuscript "Multi-Horizon Failure-Risk Models for Battery
Failure Prediction: How Much Cross-Chemistry Transfer Is a State-of-Health
Shortcut?" for consideration as a Regular Paper in the IEEE Transactions
on Reliability, with suggested category B2 (Prognostics and Health
Management); B5 (Degradation and Maintenance) is an equally suitable
secondary classification.

Rather than remaining-useful-life regression, we study multi-horizon
failure-risk classification -- whether a lithium-ion cell fails within
the next H cycles, H in {10, 20, 30, 50} -- and subject it to a
validity-focused evaluation across eight public aging datasets spanning
four chemistries (LCO, LFP, NCA, NMC) from six laboratories, four model
families, a discrete-time hazard formulation, and leakage-clean,
cross-fitted probability calibration. The central finding is a cautionary
result of direct relevance to prognostics practice: apparent
cross-dataset, cross-chemistry transfer is largely a State-of-Health
(SOH) shortcut. With SOH as a feature, transfer reaches pooled AUC up to
1.00; without it, every model class collapses toward or below chance
(at best 0.58 at H=20), and an unfitted distance-to-threshold rule on
SOH alone (0.912 AUC on the 141-cell MIT-Stanford set) matches the tuned
ensembles. Because SOH also enters the failure label, all with-SOH
numbers are diagnostic-proximity upper bounds -- quantified by
failure-definition relabeling, prognosis-only evaluation, and
same-chemistry cross-laboratory controls that attribute the collapse to
dataset shift at least as much as chemistry. The finding replicates on
60 pre-registered NMC/NCA/LFP cells from two further laboratories, and
a cost-sensitive operational evaluation shows transferred thresholds
and source-fitted calibrators do not survive shift. A complete ESP32-S3
deployment (372 kB, sub-millisecond inference) demonstrates the models
are deployable whatever they are reading.

We believe this work fits the Transactions' mission closely: it is a
reliability-oriented validity analysis of prognostic models -- with
cell-level bootstrap uncertainty as primary evidence, DeLong tests
demoted for pseudoreplication, monotonicity of cumulative-risk
estimates enforced by a survival formulation, and decision-theoretic
operating-point analysis -- rather than a battery-materials study.

Disclosure. A condensed conference version (four datasets, tree
